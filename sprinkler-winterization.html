The Short Answer

Get your system blown out before the first hard freeze.

A sprinkler blowout clears water from your irrigation lines, valves and heads using compressed air, so nothing is left behind to freeze and expand once Wenatchee Valley temperatures drop for the season. Most homeowners in the valley schedule their blowout sometime in fall, before sustained freezing weather sets in — the exact week varies year to year, which is why we work through the valley zone by zone rather than waiting for a single fixed date.

Why Winterize

Sprinkler Systems Retain Water

Underground sprinkler lines, valves, backflow devices and heads hold water even after the system is shut off for the season. Wenatchee Valley winters bring sustained freezing temperatures, and water left in those components can freeze and expand.

A seasonal blowout is intended to help reduce that risk by clearing the water out before hard freezes arrive, rather than leaving it to chance.

How It Works

How Sprinkler Blowouts Work

We isolate the irrigation system from the water supply, then use compressed air to push through each zone in sequence, clearing water from the mainline, lateral pipes, valves and every sprinkler head.

Backflow devices and above-ground components get particular attention since they're the most exposed to freeze damage. This is a service call, not a DIY procedure — the wrong air pressure or sequence can damage a system, which is why it's handled by a technician rather than a homeowner with a compressor.

What Retains Water

Parts of Your System That Can Hold Water

Mainlines and lateral pipes, control valves and valve boxes, backflow prevention devices, and the sprinkler heads themselves can all retain water after the system shuts off. Low points in the pipe run are particularly prone to holding standing water, which is why a thorough blowout works through every zone rather than just the visible heads.

Timing

When Wenatchee Homeowners Typically Prepare

Most Wenatchee Valley property owners schedule their blowout in fall, as overnight temperatures start dropping consistently and before the first sustained hard freeze. Because that timing shifts year to year, we open scheduling early in the season and work through the valley in the weeks leading up to winter.

Our Process

What Our Process Looks Like

A technician arrives, confirms the number of zones and system layout, shuts off the water supply, and connects compressed air to clear each zone in sequence. We check backflow devices and above-ground components before we leave.

Every visit is a real on-site service call — we don't quote sprinkler winterization sight unseen.

Set Expectations

What a Blowout Does — and Doesn't — Do

A blowout is intended to clear water from an irrigation system before freezing weather, reducing the risk of freeze-related damage to pipes, valves and heads. It is not a repair service — if a system already has a leak, a broken head, or a failing valve, that issue should be addressed separately. We don't make guarantees about specific freeze temperatures or claim a blowout eliminates all risk of winter damage; we do the work thoroughly and tell you honestly what we find along the way.
