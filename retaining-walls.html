Why walls fail

The wall isn't the problem. The drainage is.

We've rebuilt retaining walls that didn't make it two winters. Poor drainage behind them, wrong base, wrong block for the load. The homeowner paid twice.

Wenatchee's freeze-thaw cycle is brutal on walls without proper drainage. Water behind a wall builds hydrostatic pressure that pushes outward all winter. We put drainage aggregate and perforated pipe behind every wall we build. No exceptions.

Drainage aggregate behind every wall

Proper drainage is what separates a wall that holds from one that fails in three winters. We never skip this step.

Perforated pipe at the base

We install drainage pipe at the footing to move water away from the wall base and prevent saturation of the retained soil.

Compacted base, properly sized

A wall is only as strong as what it sits on. We excavate to undisturbed soil, compact the base, and size the footing for the height and load.

Right block or boulder for the job

Not every wall system is right for every situation. We spec the block, batter, and system for the height, slope, and soil conditions specific to your site.
