What it includes

What tree trimming covers

Trimming and pruning are about shaping a tree that stays. Common work includes crown reduction, dead wood removal, structural pruning to build a sound framework of branches, and canopy shaping for clearance and balance. Proper pruning extends a tree's life and keeps large trees from becoming future hazards.

Common reasons

Why property owners ask for trimming

  • Limbs hanging over a roof, gutter, driveway, or walkway
  • Dead, broken, or hanging branches after wind or snow
  • Storm damage that needs cleanup and a safe, balanced crown
  • Crowded or unbalanced canopies that load one side of the tree
  • Young trees that need structure while they grow
Trimming or removal

Trimming or removal: how to decide

Trimming keeps the tree standing and works best when its structure is sound. Removal takes the whole tree down, and it is usually the right call when a tree is dead, has extensive decay, or is leaning toward a structure. If you're unsure which applies, we'll look at the tree and tell you directly. For removal details, see our tree removal page.

Pruning standards

Why we don't top trees

Topping cuts a tree's main branches back to stubs. Arborists generally advise against it, because the tree tends to respond with fast, weak regrowth and open wounds that can invite decay, and topping often does not reduce long-term hazard. Good pruning maintains the tree's natural form, often by selecting a strong leader and removing crossing, dead, and competing branches.

Timing

Seasonal considerations

Timing depends on the species. Many deciduous trees are commonly pruned while dormant, but some species bleed sap heavily when cut at that time, and conifers are often pruned at other times of year. Dead, broken, or hanging limbs should be dealt with when they're found, whatever the season.

Safety

Safety first

Pruning can be dangerous. Work high in a tree, cutting near power lines, and removing limbs that could fall on people, vehicles, or buildings are jobs for trained crews with the right equipment. Keep clear of power lines, and contact your electric utility before doing any work near them.
